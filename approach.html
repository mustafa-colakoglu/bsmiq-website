Approach

AI authors. The engine decides.

We are building for a world where Claude and other models help design the business — and where a deterministic runtime still owns correctness, authority, and audit.

Where models shine

Proposing entity shapes. Naming modules. Drafting rules in clear language. Walking a founder from “we sell products with options” to a coherent set of definitions. Explaining why the compiler rejected a change.

Where models must not be the runtime

Authorizing a write. Enforcing an invariant in a transaction. Generating identifiers. Publishing events only after commit. Those are engine responsibilities — stable codes, explicit phases, no improvisation.

The loop we want

  • 1. Describe A human or an agent drafts or revises the business specification.
  • 2. Compile The engine validates the whole model and returns precise diagnostics — not a vague failure.
  • 3. Activate Modules and entity versions become part of the running system when they are ready — not when a deploy happens to ship.
  • 4. Operate Commands execute against live records under the rules the model declared. Events and audit follow.
  • 5. Learn Gaps show up as refused operations and missing definitions — signal to improve the model, not to patch random handlers.

Why Claude’s ecosystem matters to us

We want builders and agents that can reason carefully about structure, trade-offs, and long contexts — then hand a clean artifact to a system that will not “hallucinate” a permission grant at runtime. A startup program that deepens that partnership is exactly the kind of leverage bsmiq is built for.

What we will not do

We will not replace the business model with a chat transcript. We will not let generated UI become the source of truth. The specification stays reviewable, versioned, and executable — with AI as a powerful author, not an unsupervised operator.
